## Standard 1: American History — Historical Inquiry and Analysis (SS.3.A.1)

**Strand:** American History
**Standard Grouping:** Historical Inquiry and Analysis
**Purpose:** Students learn to work with primary and secondary sources, use technology to gather historical information, and develop a vocabulary for the social sciences. This is the foundational inquiry standard for Grade 3 history and connects to all content in the American History strand.

**CPALMS Standard Page:** https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewIdea/Preview/753

---

### Benchmark: SS.3.A.1.1

- **Code:** SS.3.A.1.1
- **Strand:** American History
- **Standard grouping:** SS.3.A.1 — Historical Inquiry and Analysis
- **Grade:** 3
- **Status:** State Board Approved (Date Adopted or Revised: 02/14)
- **Official description:** Analyze primary and secondary sources.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
  - (From lesson resources on CPALMS page, illustrative examples include:)
  - The Great Seal of the State of Florida (primary source photograph/visual)
  - Historical photographs related to Juneteenth, Independence Day, Veterans Day, and other patriotic holidays
  - Florida state song lyrics ("Swanee River") as a primary source
  - Biographies and summary history texts as secondary sources
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly stated.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred from extensive lesson plan resources) Students develop the ability to read, examine, and draw conclusions from both primary sources (original documents, photographs, artifacts from the time) and secondary sources (summaries, biographies, textbooks about the time). They learn to distinguish between the two types and recognize why each has value.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
  - (Inferred) Students may assume secondary sources are more reliable because they are easier to read; may not understand why the distinction between primary and secondary matters for historical reasoning.
- **Instructional tasks or items:** (From CPALMS lesson examples.)
  - Examine a primary source photograph + read a secondary source summary → answer questions comparing both sources.
  - Sort a deck of cards into primary source / secondary source categories.
  - Analyze the Great Seal of Florida as a primary source and explain its significance.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated. (Implied: primary source, secondary source.)
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Social Studies Grade 3 (5021050)
  - Access Social Studies - Grade 3 (7721014)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 3 (5011030)
  - **Access Point:** SS.3.A.1.AP.1 — Identify and use primary and secondary sources to obtain information.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/2961)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify whether a source is primary or secondary and explain what makes it valuable for understanding a historical event?
  - Misconception to detect: Treating all documents as equally reliable; confusing a summary of a letter (secondary) with the letter itself (primary).
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer history investigation arc — a character uncovers a historical photograph and a newspaper article about the same event; learner must compare the two sources and identify what each reveals that the other does not.
  - Learner evidence: Correctly identifies source type; explains at least one strength of each source for historical understanding; draws at least one conclusion from the sources.

- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**

## Standard 2: Geography — The World in Spatial Terms (SS.3.G.1)

**Strand:** Geography
**Standard Grouping:** The World in Spatial Terms
**Purpose:** Students use maps and geographic tools to gather information, analyze geographic data, and understand how location and spatial relationships shape human decisions and experiences. This is the foundational geographic literacy standard for Grade 3.

---

### Benchmark: SS.3.G.1.1

- **Code:** SS.3.G.1.1
- **Strand:** Geography
- **Standard grouping:** SS.3.G.1 — The World in Spatial Terms
- **Grade:** 3
- **Status:** State Board Approved (Date Adopted or Revised: 02/14)
- **Official description:** Use thematic maps, tables, charts, graphs, and photos to analyze geographic information.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly stated.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred) Students develop geographic literacy by reading multiple types of visual representations (not just political maps) that convey geographic data — population, climate, land use, elevation, etc. They practice drawing conclusions from these representations.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
  - (Inferred) Students may not know how to read a thematic map (confusing it with a reference map); may treat a photo as a decoration rather than a source of geographic data.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated. (Implied: thematic map, table, chart, graph, photo as evidence.)
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Social Studies Grade 3 (5021050)
  - Access Social Studies - Grade 3 (7721014)
  - **Access Point:** SS.3.G.1.AP.1 — Use a thematic map or chart to identify selected geographic information, such as land and body of water on a map or population on a chart.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/2964)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner read a thematic map, chart, or graph and extract specific geographic information to answer a question?
  - Misconception to detect: Reading only labels without interpreting patterns or trends; not knowing what a thematic map's key is telling them.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer expedition or planning scene — a character presents a thematic map (e.g., population density, resource location, weather patterns) and the learner must extract at least two pieces of geographic information to advise a decision.
  - Learner evidence: Accurately reads the map or chart; states at least two conclusions from the geographic data; connects the geographic information to the story decision.

### Benchmark: SS.3.G.1.2

- **Code:** SS.3.G.1.2
- **Strand:** Geography
- **Standard grouping:** SS.3.G.1 — The World in Spatial Terms
- **Grade:** 3
- **Status:** State Board Approved (Date Adopted or Revised: 02/14)
- **Official description:** Review basic map elements (coordinate grid, cardinal and intermediate directions, title, compass rose, scale, key/legend with symbols).
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
  - (From lesson resources on CPALMS: students identify map elements and use a map scale to estimate distances between landmarks.)
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly stated.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred) Students confirm and apply their knowledge of the standard components of a map — understanding what each element communicates and how to use it to read a map accurately.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
  - (Inferred) Students may not distinguish between cardinal (N, S, E, W) and intermediate (NE, SE, SW, NW) directions; may ignore the map scale when estimating distances; may overlook the key when interpreting symbols.
- **Glossary terms:** (Implied from benchmark.) coordinate grid, cardinal directions, intermediate directions, title, compass rose, scale, key/legend, symbols.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: reviews Grade 2 or earlier map skills; builds toward more complex geographic analysis in later grades.)
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Social Studies Grade 3 (5021050)
  - Access Social Studies - Grade 3 (7721014)
  - **Access Point:** SS.3.G.1.AP.2 — Identify elements on a map, such as title, key/legend, cardinal directions, compass rose, and coordinate grid.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/2965)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner correctly use all major map elements (compass rose, scale, key, coordinate grid) to locate places and answer geographic questions?
  - Misconception to detect: Misreading intermediate directions (e.g., confusing NE with N); not using the scale to estimate real distances; ignoring the legend when interpreting colored regions or symbols.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer navigation scene — a character hands the learner a map of a story world and asks them to identify where something is located, how far away it is, and what direction to travel; learner must use the compass rose, scale, and key to answer.
  - Learner evidence: Correctly identifies at least 4 map elements; uses the scale to make a distance estimate; reads the legend to identify what a map symbol represents; uses a cardinal or intermediate direction correctly.

## Product Implementation Guidance

### Primer Story Arc Suggestion — Social Studies

**Arc title:** "The Town Council" (historical inquiry and geography arc)
**Anchor problem:** A fictional town needs to make an important decision about its future — and the learner, as a town council advisor, must use primary and secondary sources and geographic tools to recommend the best course of action.

| Chapter | Focus benchmark(s) | Scene type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | SS.3.A.1.3 — Meet the five council advisors (historian, geographer, economist, civic leader, government official) | Vocabulary/role introduction |
| 2 | SS.3.A.1.1 — A historical photograph and a written account of what happened — analyze both | Primary vs. secondary source scene |
| 3 | SS.3.A.1.2 — The historian asks the learner to look up more sources in the town archive | Digital source gathering |
| 4 | SS.3.G.1.2 — A map of the town: use the elements to find where the problem is occurring | Map reading scene |
| 5 | SS.3.G.1.1 — A thematic map showing resource distribution: advise the council | Thematic map analysis |
| 6 | SS.3.A.1.1 — Return to the sources: what conclusion do they support? | Evidence synthesis / culminating argument |
