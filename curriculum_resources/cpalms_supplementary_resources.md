## Resource 1: Appendix B — Reading

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.R.1.4 (poem form examples), ELA.3.R.2.1 (text complexity as implied reference)
- **Source URL:** https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixb.pdf
- **Fetch status:** ✅ Full content retrieved
- **Contents:**

  Appendix B is the Florida B.E.S.T. ELA framework's master reading reference document. It covers three main areas:

  **1. Text Complexity Framework (pp. 148–153 approx.)**
  Defines the three-component model: *qualitative* (reader-and-task factors), *quantitative* (measurable data), and *student-centered* measures. Includes the official Quantitative Grade Band chart:

  | Grade Level | Flesch-Kincaid | Lexile |
  |---|---|---|
  | K–1st | -1.3 – 2.18 | BR – 430L |
  | 2nd–3rd | 1.98 – 5.34 | 420L – 820L |
  | 4th–5th | 4.51 – 7.73 | 740L – 1010L |
  | 6th–8th | 6.51 – 10.34 | 925L – 1185L |
  | 9th–10th | 8.32 – 12.12 | 1050L – 1335L |
  | 11th–12th | 10.34 – 14.2 | 1185L – 1385L |

  Includes a full Text Complexity Rubric for teacher evaluation of qualitative and quantitative factors together.

  **2. Sample Literary Texts by Grade Band**
  Lists example literary texts (titles and authors) at each grade band to illustrate text complexity expectations. Grade 3 falls in the 2nd–3rd band (420L–820L Lexile range).

  **3. Poem Form Examples**
  This is the direct reference for ELA.3.R.1.4. Contains labeled examples of all four required poem types:
  - **Free verse** — no rhyme scheme or fixed meter
  - **Rhymed verse** — consistent end-rhyme pattern
  - **Haiku** — three lines, 5–7–5 syllable structure, seasonal/nature subject
  - **Limerick** — five lines, AABBA rhyme scheme, humorous tone

  **Primer relevance:** Use the Lexile band (420L–820L) as a target for Grade 3 Primer story text complexity. Poem form examples can be used as literal models when building story segments that include poetry (see R.1.4 incorporation notes).

## Resource 2: Appendix E — Reading Foundations

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.F.1.4 (fluency norms and oral reading rubrics), ELA.3.F.1.3 (foundational reading background)
- **Source URL:** https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixe.pdf
- **Fetch status:** ✅ Full content retrieved
- **Contents:**

  Appendix E is the Florida B.E.S.T. ELA framework's master reference for foundational reading skills. It is the most technically dense appendix in the series.

  **1. Introduction to Reading Foundations (p. 206)**
  Explains the structured literacy framework underpinning the F strand: explicit, sequential, systematic, cumulative instruction across phonological awareness, phonics, morphology, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension. Emphasizes multisensory learning pathways.

  **2. Dolch Word Lists (pp. 206–207)**
  Provides the complete Dolch sight word lists by grade level:
  - Pre-Primer (~40 words: a, and, away, big, blue, can, come, down, find...)
  - Primer (~100 words)
  - 1st Grade (~100 words)
  - 2nd Grade (~100 words)

  **3. Fry Word Lists (pp. 207–208)**
  Provides the first 200 Fry high-frequency words, organized into 1st hundred and 2nd hundred, used for fluency and sight word assessment.

  **4. Fluency Norms — Hasbrouck-Tindal Chart (p. 209)**
  The official WCPM (words correct per minute) norms by grade, time of year, and percentile rank. **Grade 3 norms:**

  | Percentile | Fall WCPM | Winter WCPM | Spring WCPM |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 90th | 134 | 161 | 166 |
  | 75th | 104 | 137 | 139 |
  | 50th | 83 | 97 | 112 |
  | 25th | 59 | 79 | 91 |
  | 10th | 40 | 62 | 63 |

  Source: Hasbrouck & Tindal (2017), University of Oregon.

  **5. Oral Reading Fluency Rubrics (pp. 209–210)**
  Two rubric systems for evaluating oral reading fluency quality (beyond just WCPM):
  - **NAEP Fluency Scale** (Levels 1–4): rates phrasing, syntax preservation, and expressive interpretation
  - **Multidimensional Fluency Scale** (1–4): rates Expression & Volume, Phrasing, Smoothness, and Pace separately

  **6. Interventions for Secondary Students (p. 211)**
  Three learner profiles for non-proficient decoders (irregular gaps, ELL, persistent difficulties). Not directly applicable to Grade 3 but provides context for differentiation.

  **7. Glossary of Terms (pp. 211–219)**
  A comprehensive ~100-term glossary covering all ELA-specific vocabulary used in the B.E.S.T. standards, with definitions and synonyms. Terms include: accuracy, affix, allegory, alphabetic principle, blending, central idea, claim, clarify, comprehend, connotation, decoding, digraph, diphthong, encoding, fluency, grapheme, morpheme, morphology, onset-rime, orthography, phoneme, phonological awareness, plot, point of view, prosody, syntax, theme, tone, topic, transition, universal theme, vowel team, and ~70 more.

  **Primer relevance:**
  - The Grade 3 WCPM norms are the benchmark for F.1.4 fluency assessment tasks
  - The glossary is a direct reference for accurate use of ELA terminology throughout all Primer content
  - Dolch/Fry lists inform which sight words should be treated as known vs. potentially new for Grade 3 Primer texts

## Resource 3: Writing Types (Appendix A)

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.C.1.1 (narrative writing), ELA.3.C.1.2 (opinion writing), ELA.3.C.1.3 (expository writing)
- **Source URL:** https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixa.pdf *(URL not yet verified via fetch; follows naming convention of confirmed appendices B and E)*
- **Fetch status:** ⚠️ Not yet fetched — add URL to a future session message to retrieve content
- **Contents (based on FL BEST framework documentation):**

  Appendix A defines the three writing types used throughout the B.E.S.T. ELA standards:

  - **Narrative writing** — tells a real or fictional story with a beginning, middle, and end; includes story elements such as character, setting, plot, dialogue, and descriptive detail
  - **Opinion/argumentative writing** — states a claim or opinion, provides reasons supported by evidence, and includes a conclusion
  - **Expository/informational writing** — presents factual information about a topic in an organized way with an introduction, body paragraphs, elaborative techniques, and a conclusion

  Each type includes grade-band progressions showing what is expected at each grade level, scaffolding from K through 12.

  **Primer relevance:** The writing type definitions directly determine the genre expected in C.1.1, C.1.2, and C.1.3 assessment tasks. Primer narrative prompts for C.1.1 must meet the narrative writing type criteria; opinion prompts for C.1.2 must include a claim + reasons structure.

## Resource 4: Elaborative Techniques (Appendix C)

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.C.1.3 (expository writing)
- **Source URL:** https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixc.pdf *(URL not yet verified via fetch)*
- **Fetch status:** ⚠️ Not yet fetched
- **Contents (based on FL BEST framework documentation):**

  Appendix C defines specific elaborative techniques students are expected to use when writing expository or argumentative text. Techniques include:
  - **Definition** — explaining what a term or concept means
  - **Description** — providing sensory or specific details
  - **Comparison** — showing how two things are alike or different
  - **Example** — giving a concrete instance to illustrate a point
  - **Cause and effect** — explaining why something happens or what results from it
  - **Anecdote** — using a brief story to support a point

  Grade-level progressions show which techniques are introduced, practiced, and mastered at each grade.

  **Primer relevance:** For C.1.3 assessment, Primer should prompt learners to use at least one of these named techniques. Explicit instruction in the Primer guide character's voice can model technique use.

## Resource 5: Elementary Figurative Language (Appendix D)

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.V.1.1 (vocabulary — figurative language), ELA.3.R.3.1 (reading across genres — cross-genre comparisons that include figurative uses)
- **Source URL:** https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixd.pdf *(URL not yet verified via fetch)*
- **Fetch status:** ⚠️ Not yet fetched
- **Contents (based on FL BEST framework documentation):**

  Appendix D catalogues the figurative language devices taught in elementary grades, with definitions and examples. Devices expected at Grade 3 typically include:
  - **Simile** — comparison using "like" or "as"
  - **Metaphor** — direct comparison without "like" or "as"
  - **Personification** — giving human qualities to non-human things
  - **Hyperbole** — extreme exaggeration for effect
  - **Alliteration** — repetition of initial consonant sounds
  - **Idiom** — an expression whose meaning differs from literal meaning
  - **Onomatopoeia** — words that imitate sounds

  **Primer relevance:** V.1.1 explicitly expects students to interpret figurative language in context. Primer texts can deliberately embed these devices in story passages to create natural assessment moments.

## Resource 6: Context Clues and Word Relationships

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.V.1.1
- **Source URL:** Likely a CPALMS-hosted PDF at: https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/ (filename unknown) — needs research
- **Fetch status:** ⚠️ Not yet fetched; URL pattern unknown
- **Contents (based on FL BEST framework documentation):**

  This reference defines the context clue strategies students use when encountering unknown words:
  - **Definition context clues** — the text directly defines the word
  - **Synonym context clues** — a nearby word has a similar meaning
  - **Antonym context clues** — a nearby word has the opposite meaning
  - **Example context clues** — examples in the text illustrate meaning
  - **Inference context clues** — reader infers meaning from surrounding ideas

  Word relationship types also covered: synonyms, antonyms, multiple-meaning words, analogies.

  **Primer relevance:** V.1.1 assessment moments should embed unknown vocabulary in passages with deliberate context clues of varying types, creating opportunities to observe which strategies learners apply.

## Resource 9: Convention Progression by Grade Level

- **Referenced by:** ELA.3.C.3.1 (following grammar conventions)
- **Source URL:** Likely a CPALMS-hosted PDF — URL pattern unknown
- **Fetch status:** ⚠️ Not yet fetched
- **Contents (based on FL BEST framework documentation):**

  A K–12 scope-and-sequence table showing which grammar and mechanics conventions are introduced, practiced, and mastered at each grade level. For Grade 3, "mastery" skills include:
  - Singular and plural nouns (including irregular plurals: *child/children*, *mouse/mice*)
  - Capitalization of proper nouns and first words in sentences
  - End punctuation (. ? !)
  - Commas in dates and to separate items in a series
  - Quotation marks for dialogue (Grade 3 level)
  - Correct use of *a/an*

  "Implementation but not yet mastery" skills for Grade 3 include:
  - Subject-verb agreement with intervening clauses
  - Fragments and run-ons
  - Conjunctions, principal modals, appositives, main/subordinate clauses

  **Primer relevance:** C.3.1 Primer tasks should only assess mastery-level conventions at Grade 3. The convention progression table is the authoritative source for which skills to hold learners accountable for at this grade.
