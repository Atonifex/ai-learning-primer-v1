**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Across Genres
**Purpose:** Students apply reading skills across both literary and informational genres. They identify and interpret figurative language, summarize texts, and compare how different authors approach the same topic or theme.

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.3.1
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.3 — Reading Across Genres
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Identify and explain metaphors, personification, and hyperbole in text(s).
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: In addition to the types of figurative language listed in this benchmark, students are still working with types from previous grades such as simile, alliteration, and idiom. Other examples can be used in instruction.
  - Clarification 2: See Elementary Figurative Language.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Students identify and explain three new figurative language types (metaphor, personification, hyperbole) while continuing to work with previously learned types (simile, alliteration, idiom).
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: confusing simile and metaphor; treating all exaggeration as hyperbole without examining the author's intent.)
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends simile/alliteration/idiom from prior grades; leads to more complex figurative language analysis in Grade 4+.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.3.AP.1 — Identify metaphors, personification and hyperbole in text(s).
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15215)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify a metaphor, personification, or hyperbole in a story passage and explain what it means?
  - Misconception to detect: Treating a simile as a metaphor (missing the "like/as" distinction); identifying any big statement as hyperbole without checking for intentional exaggeration.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer story where a character speaks in vivid figurative language (e.g., a character who "was a thundercloud stomping through the hall" or says "I've told you a million times"); learner identifies and labels the device.
  - Learner evidence: Names the figurative device correctly and explains what it means in plain language.
