## Standard 1: Reading — Reading Prose and Poetry (ELA.3.R.1)

**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Prose and Poetry
**Purpose:** Students read and analyze literary texts including stories, plays, and poems. They examine character development, theme, perspective, figurative language, and poetic forms.

---

### Benchmark: ELA.3.R.1.1

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.1.1
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Explain how one or more characters develop throughout the plot in a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: When explaining character development, students will include character traits, feelings, motivations, and responses to situations.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred) Students examine how characters change or stay the same across events in a plot, using textual evidence about their traits, feelings, and motivations.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
- **Related courses or access points:**

  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 3 (5011030)

  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 3 (5010014)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.1.AP.1 — Identify how a character develops throughout the plot in a literary text.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15207)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify and explain how a story character's traits, feelings, or motivations change from the beginning to the end of a story segment?
  - Misconception to detect: Describing only what a character did (plot summary) rather than how the character changed or why.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer story arcs with a central guide character who evolves across chapters; learner reflects on a character's shifting behavior in an adventure, mystery, or community scenario.
  - Learner evidence: Written or spoken explanation naming a trait, citing a moment in the text where it changed, and connecting it to the character's feelings or motivation.

### Benchmark: ELA.3.R.1.2

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.1.2
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Explain a theme and how it develops, using details, in a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred from lesson plan resources on CPALMS page.)
  - Students identify the larger message, lesson, or moral of a text and explain how specific details (events, character actions, dialogue) support and develop that theme.
  - Graphic organizers connecting details → theme are a noted instructional approach.
  - Paired with character development (ELA.3.R.1.1) to show how character actions and changes contribute to theme.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 3 (5011030)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.1.AP.2 — Identify a theme and how it develops, using details, in a literary text.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15208)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner state the theme of a story and identify at least two details from the text that helped develop it?
  - Misconception to detect: Confusing the topic ("friendship") with the theme as a complete idea ("True friendship means helping someone even when it is hard"); identifying only the ending moral rather than showing how the whole story built to it.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer story chapters with embedded moral tension (e.g., a character chooses between self-interest and community good); learner is asked what lesson the story teaches and to find evidence across scenes.
  - Learner evidence: Articulates a theme as a complete statement; cites specific details from beginning, middle, and end of the story.

### Benchmark: ELA.3.R.1.3

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.1.3
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Explain different characters' perspectives in a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: The term perspective means "a particular attitude toward or way of regarding something." The term point of view is used when referring to the person of the narrator. This is to prevent confusion and conflation.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred) Students identify how two or more characters see the same situation differently based on their backgrounds, goals, or feelings, and explain why those differences exist.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Common inferred: confusing "perspective" with "point of view" — the clarification directly addresses this.)
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.1.AP.3 — Identify different characters' perspectives in a literary text.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15209)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify how two characters see the same event differently and explain why?
  - Misconception to detect: Equating a character's perspective with the narrator's point of view; assuming one perspective is simply "correct."
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A two-character conflict in a Primer story where each character has a different goal or background (e.g., a dispute over resources, a misunderstanding between a villager and an outsider); learner asked to explain each side.
  - Learner evidence: Identifies at least two distinct perspectives; explains what each character wants or believes and how that shapes their reaction.

## Standard 2: Reading — Reading Informational Text (ELA.3.R.2)

**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Informational Text
**Purpose:** Students read and analyze informational texts. They examine text features, text structure (chronology, comparison, cause/effect), central idea and relevant details, author's purpose, and author's claims and evidence.

---

### Benchmark: ELA.3.R.2.1

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.2.1
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.2 — Reading Informational Text
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Explain how text features contribute to meaning and identify the text structures of chronology, comparison, and cause/effect in texts.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly stated.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred from lesson resources) Students identify how specific text features (headings, captions, timelines, charts) help a reader understand content; they also identify the organizational pattern the author used and explain how it shapes meaning.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 3 (5011030)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.2.AP.1 — Identify the text structures of chronological order, comparison and cause/effect in texts.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15211)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner name the organizational structure of a short informational passage and explain how a text feature (e.g., a map, a timeline) adds to understanding?
  - Misconception to detect: Confusing text structure type — especially mixing up "comparison" and "cause/effect" when both involve two things being related.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer presents a short informational excerpt embedded in a story scene (e.g., an investigator reads a timeline of events or a comparison chart of two towns); learner identifies the structure and one contributing text feature.
  - Learner evidence: Names the structure correctly; explains what the text feature shows and why it matters.

### Benchmark: ELA.3.R.2.2

- **Code:** ELA.3.R.2.2
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.3.R.2 — Reading Informational Text
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Identify the central idea and explain how relevant details support that idea in a text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly stated.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
  - (Inferred from extensive lesson resources) Students practice the two-step process of: (1) determining what the text is mostly about (central idea) and (2) identifying which details from the text are relevant — meaning they actually support the central idea and are not just interesting facts.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
  - (Common inferred) Students may identify the topic (a single word or phrase) rather than articulating the central idea as a complete statement; they may also include interesting but non-supporting details as "relevant."
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Three (5010044)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 3 (7710014)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 3 (5011030)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.3.R.2.AP.2 — Identify the central idea and select relevant details that support that idea in a text.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified — description confirmed via CPALMS search (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/Preview/15212) and Twinkl resource; print page 15212 returned with access point info.
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner state the central idea as a complete idea (not just a topic word) and identify 2–3 details that support it?
  - Misconception to detect: Writing a topic (e.g., "dolphins") instead of a central idea (e.g., "Dolphins are highly intelligent animals that communicate in complex ways"); including irrelevant interesting facts.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer presents a short informational insert in a story chapter (e.g., a field guide page on an animal, a news article about a discovery); learner identifies the central idea and supporting details.
  - Learner evidence: States a central idea as a complete sentence; selects 2–3 details and explains why each is relevant (not just interesting).
