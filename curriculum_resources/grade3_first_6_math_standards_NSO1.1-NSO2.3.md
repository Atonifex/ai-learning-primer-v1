## Standard 1: Number Sense and Operations — Place Value and Number Forms (MA.3.NSO.1)

**Strand:** Number Sense and Operations
**Standard Grouping:** Understand place value through ten-thousands; represent, compare, and order whole numbers.
**CPALMS Standard Page:** https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewIdea/Preview/752

---

### Benchmark: MA.3.NSO.1.1

- **Code:** MA.3.NSO.1.1
- **Strand:** Number Sense and Operations
- **Standard grouping:** MA.3.NSO.1 — Place Value and Number Forms
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Read and write numbers from 0 to 10,000 using standard form, expanded form and word form.
- **Official examples:**
  - The number two thousand five hundred thirty written in standard form is 2,530 and in expanded form is 2,000 + 500 + 30.
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly listed.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:**
  - The purpose is for students to express numbers in standard form, expanded form, and word form. This extends Grade 2 work reading and writing numbers within 1,000 (MA.2.NSO.2.1).
  - Students learn to express multi-digit whole numbers using place value of digits to name them in words (e.g., "two thousand five hundred thirty" is named from the 2 in the thousands place, the 5 in the hundreds place, and the 3 in the tens place).
  - Students express multi-digit whole numbers by decomposing by place value, showing them as an addition expression with the value of each nonzero digit (e.g., 2,530 → 2,000 + 500 + 30).
  - Decomposing numbers in expanded form builds understanding of addition/subtraction algorithms and the distributive property in multiplication.
  - Teachers should ensure practice in both vertical and horizontal forms, including use of commutative and associative properties (MTR.2.1, MTR.3.1, MTR.6.1).
- **Common misconceptions or errors:**
  - When a digit in a multi-digit number is 0, students may misunderstand it as nothing rather than 0 of that place value (e.g., in 2,530, there are 0 ones; in 1,008, there are 0 hundreds and 0 tens).
- **Instructional tasks or items:** (See NSO.1.2 for more developed task examples.)
- **Glossary terms:**
  - Expression
  - Whole number
- **Vertical alignment:**
  - Previous: MA.2.NSO.2.1 — Read and write numbers within 1,000 using standard form, expanded form, and word form.
  - Next: MA.3.NSO.1.2 (place value decomposition); MA.3.NSO.2.1 (addition/subtraction using place value understanding).
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Grade Three Mathematics (5012050)
  - Access Mathematics Grade 3 (7712040)
  - Grade 3 Accelerated Mathematics (5012055)
  - Foundational Skills in Mathematics 3–5 (5012015)
  - **Access Point:** MA.3.NSO.1.AP.1 — Read and generate numbers from 0 to 1,000 using standard form and expanded form.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (CPALMS PrintStandard page retrieved directly: https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15307)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner translate between the three representations of a number?
  - Misconception to detect: Treating a zero digit as non-existent rather than as a placeholder with value.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A storekeeper counting inventory (e.g., "We have 2,530 items — how do you write that on the receipt?"); a map with coordinates using 4-digit numbers; a historical ledger reading activity.
  - Learner evidence: Accurately writes the same number in all three forms; explains the meaning of each digit in context.

### Benchmark: MA.3.NSO.1.2

- **Code:** MA.3.NSO.1.2
- **Strand:** Number Sense and Operations
- **Standard grouping:** MA.3.NSO.1 — Place Value and Number Forms
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Compose and decompose four-digit numbers in multiple ways using thousands, hundreds, tens and ones. Demonstrate each composition or decomposition using objects, drawings and expressions or equations.
- **Official examples:**
  - 5,783 can be expressed as 5 thousands + 7 hundreds + 8 tens + 3 ones, or as 56 hundreds + 183 ones.
  - 1,243 = 1 thousand + 2 hundreds + 4 tens + 3 ones = 12 hundreds + 4 tens + 3 ones.
  - 5,783 as 5 thousands + 6 hundreds + 18 tens + 3 ones (regrouping 1 hundred as 10 tens).
- **Clarifications:** None explicitly labeled, but instructional tasks serve this role:
  - Part A: Express 5,783 using only hundreds and ones.
  - Part B: Express 5,783 using only thousands and hundreds.
  - Part C: Express 5,783 using only tens and ones.
  - Decompose 6,905 in two different ways.
  - Select all ways that express 8,709: 8,000+600+19; 8,000+700+9; 879 ones; 8 thousands+6 hundreds+10 tens+9 ones.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:**
  - The purpose is for students to identify that numbers can be written flexibly using decomposition, and that number sense is built on understanding place value.
  - Extends Grade 2 compose/decompose of three-digit numbers (MA.2.NSO.1.2); connects to Grade 3 addition/subtraction (MA.3.NSO.2.1).
  - Students explore the relationship between adjacent place values (1,000 = 10 hundreds; 1 hundred = 10 tens).
  - Students use objects (base ten blocks), drawings, and expressions side-by-side to compare representations.
  - Students should see examples with zero as a digit.
  - Flexibility of place value is a prerequisite for conceptual understanding of the standard algorithm for addition/subtraction with regrouping.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:**
  - Students may think the 5 in 57 represents 5 rather than 50 (5 tens). Practice with both grouping (base ten blocks) and non-grouping (counters) manipulatives helps.
  - Students may not understand that decomposing in multiple ways does not change the total value (e.g., 879 = 87 tens + 9 ones = 8 hundreds + 79 ones).
- **Instructional tasks or items:** (Multiple tasks listed above under examples.)
- **Glossary terms:**
  - Expression
  - Whole numbers
  - Equal sign
  - Equation
- **Vertical alignment:**
  - Previous: MA.2.NSO.1.2 — Compose and decompose three-digit numbers using hundreds, tens and ones.
  - Next: MA.3.NSO.2.1 — Add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using a standard algorithm.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Grade Three Mathematics (5012050)
  - Access Mathematics Grade 3 (7712040)
  - Grade 3 Accelerated Mathematics (5012055)
  - Foundational Skills in Mathematics 3–5 (5012015)
  - **Access Point:** MA.3.NSO.1.AP.2 — Compose and decompose three-digit numbers using hundreds, tens and ones. Demonstrate with objects, drawings, expressions, or equations.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15308)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner flexibly regroup a four-digit number in at least two different ways?
  - Misconception to detect: Treating one representation as the only "correct" decomposition; confusion when regrouping across place values.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A merchant repacking crates (1 crate = 10 boxes = 100 items); a market inventory scene where the learner needs to reorganize supply counts; a game where base-ten "currency" must be exchanged for different denominations.
  - Learner evidence: Produces two or more correct decompositions; explains why different forms have the same value.

### Benchmark: MA.3.NSO.1.3

- **Code:** MA.3.NSO.1.3
- **Strand:** Number Sense and Operations
- **Standard grouping:** MA.3.NSO.1 — Place Value and Number Forms
- **Grade:** 3
- **Official description:** Plot, order and compare whole numbers up to 10,000.
- **Official examples:**
  - 3,475; 4,743; and 4,753 arranged in ascending order: 3,475; 4,743; 4,753.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: When comparing, instruction includes using an appropriately scaled number line and using place values of the thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones digits.
  - Clarification 2: Number lines scaled by 50s, 100s, or 1,000s must be provided and can represent any range of numbers.
  - Clarification 3: The expectation is to use symbols (<, >, or =).
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page; inferred from clarifications and lesson resources.)
  - Students apply place value understanding to position, sequence, and compare four-digit numbers on scaled number lines.
  - Instruction should include both vertical and horizontal comparison contexts.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Not explicitly stated on page (inferred: extends from ordering numbers within 1,000 in Grade 2).
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Grade Three Mathematics (5012050)
  - Access Mathematics Grade 3 (7712040)
  - Grade 3 Accelerated Mathematics (5012055)
  - Foundational Skills in Mathematics 3–5 (5012015)
  - **Access Point:** MA.3.NSO.1.AP.3 — Plot, order and compare whole numbers up to 1,000.
