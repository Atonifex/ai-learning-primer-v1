## Standard 1: Reading — Reading Prose and Poetry (ELA.5.R.1)

**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Prose and Poetry
**Purpose:** Students analyze literary texts with increased analytical depth. They move from explaining how individual story elements work to analyzing how setting, events, conflict, and characterization collectively construct a plot; trace the development of stated or implied themes; describe how authors deliberately develop a character's perspective; and explain how multiple figurative language devices and poetic elements work together in a poem.

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.1.1

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.1.1
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Analyze how setting, events, conflict, and characterization contribute to the plot in a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Grade 5 elevates from Grade 4's "explain" to "analyze" — students examine the interplay of all four elements simultaneously, showing how each one shapes, drives, or complicates the plot. - **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner analyze a story passage and explain specifically how the setting, a key conflict, a character's traits, and a plot event each shape what happens — and how they interact with each other?

  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer story arc where the setting (an isolated mountain town in winter) creates a constraint, a conflict (a critical bridge is damaged) creates urgency, and a character's resourcefulness is what resolves the problem — learner analyzes how all four elements are interdependent.
  - Learner evidence: Written analysis naming all four elements, explaining each one's specific contribution, and connecting at least two elements to show their interaction.

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.1.2

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.1.2
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Explain the development of stated or implied theme(s) throughout a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**

- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends ELA.4.R.1.2 (explain development of stated or implied theme in a single text); Grade 5 adds explicit multi-text and multiple-theme scope.
- **Related courses or access points:**

  - Introduction to Debate Grade 5 (5010105)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.R.1.AP.2 — Show the development of a stated or implied theme in a literary text.
- **Supplementary references:** None directly cited on CPALMS page.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15007)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner articulate an implied theme (one never stated directly) as a complete thematic statement, then trace its development using specific evidence from early, middle, and late in the text?

  - Learner evidence: A complete thematic statement as a sentence; at least three cited evidence moments from beginning, middle, and end; brief explanation of how each moment advances the theme.

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.1.3

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.1.3
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Describe how an author develops a character's perspective in a literary text.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: The term perspective means "a particular attitude toward or way of regarding something."
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Grade 5 shifts from identifying different characters' perspectives (Grade 3) and comparing their development (Grade 4) to analyzing *how the author deliberately crafts* a character's perspective through word choice, dialogue, actions, and narrative position. The question shifts from "what does this character think?" to "how does the author show us what this character thinks?"
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: describing a character's personality rather than their specific perspective on events; not connecting the perspective to authorial craft choices; confusing perspective with point of view.)
- **Glossary terms:** perspective ("a particular attitude toward or way of regarding something").
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends ELA.4.R.1.3 (compare/contrast two characters' perspectives); Grade 5 focuses on a single character's perspective and how the author constructs it.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Five (5010046)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 5 (7710016)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - Introduction to Debate Grade 5 (5010105)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.R.1.AP.3 — Identify a character's perspective at different points in a literary text.
- **Supplementary references:** None directly cited on CPALMS page.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15008)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify a character's specific perspective at different points in the story and explain which authorial choices (specific word choices, dialogue, or narrative framing) reveal that perspective?
  - Misconception to detect: Describing what the character does rather than what the author does to show the perspective; treating perspective as a fixed trait rather than something that develops.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer story where a main character's perspective on outsiders shifts from suspicion to trust — learner identifies the authorial choices (which details the narrator emphasizes, how the character's internal monologue is written) that make that perspective visible.
  - Learner evidence: Names the character's perspective at two or more points; cites a specific authorial technique (e.g., "the author shows her suspicion through the word 'lurking' in how she describes the stranger's movements"); explains how the perspective develops.

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.1.4

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.1.4
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.1 — Reading Prose and Poetry
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Explain how figurative language and other poetic elements work together in a poem.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: Figurative language for the purposes of this benchmark refers to metaphor, simile, alliteration, personification, hyperbole, imagery, and idiom. Other examples can be used in instruction.
  - Clarification 2: Poetic elements to be used for the purposes of this benchmark are form, rhyme, meter, line breaks, and imagery.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Grade 5 moves from identifying poem forms (Grade 3) to analyzing how figurative language devices and structural poetic elements (form, rhyme, meter, line breaks) work *together* to create a unified effect or meaning. The emphasis is on interplay: how a line break enhances a metaphor, or how meter reinforces a hyperbolic claim.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: treating figurative language and poetic structure as separate, parallel lists rather than as interacting forces; analyzing each device in isolation without explaining how they combine to create a single effect.)
- **Glossary terms:** metaphor, simile, alliteration, personification, hyperbole, idiom; form, rhyme, meter, line breaks, imagery.
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends ELA.4.R.1.4 (analyze how structural elements of a poem contribute to meaning); Grade 5 adds figurative language as an explicit co-element.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Five (5010046)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 5 (7710016)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - Introduction to Debate Grade 5 (5010105)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.R.1.AP.4 — Explain how figurative language and imagery work together in a poem.
- **Supplementary references:**
  - **Elementary Figurative Language (Appendix D):** Referenced indirectly through the figurative language list. Defines all seven device types (metaphor, simile, alliteration, personification, hyperbole, idiom, imagery) with examples. See `cpalms_supplementary_resources.md`, Resource 5.
  - Source URL: https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixd.pdf
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15009)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner select one figurative device and one poetic structural element from a poem and explain specifically how they work together to create a single effect?
  - Misconception to detect: Listing devices separately ("this poem has alliteration and rhyme") without explaining how they interact; treating structure and figurative language as decorative rather than meaning-making.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer story includes a poem written by a character — learner reads it and explains how the broken line structure and a central metaphor work together to mirror the character's emotional state.
  - Learner evidence: Names one figurative device and one structural element; explains their interaction in two to three sentences; ties the interaction to the poem's overall effect or meaning.

## Standard 2: Reading — Reading Informational Text (ELA.5.R.2)

**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Informational Text
**Purpose:** Students analyze informational texts with growing sophistication. They explain how text structures and features create overall meaning, explain how relevant details support central ideas (including implied ones), analyze author purpose and perspective, and track the full structure of an argument — from claim to evidence to reasoning.

---

- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.2 — Reading Informational Text

  - Learner evidence: Names the text structure and at least one text feature; explains specifically how each contributes to the overall meaning (not just "it helps the reader understand").

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.2.4

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.2.4
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.2 — Reading Informational Text
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Track the development of an argument, identifying the specific claim(s), evidence, and reasoning.

- **Glossary terms:** claim ("a statement that asserts something is true").
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends ELA.4.R.2.4 (explain claim, reasons, and evidence); Grade 5 adds explicit "reasoning" and "tracking development" as the analytical lens.
- **Related courses or access points:**

  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - Introduction to Debate Grade 5 (5010105)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.R.2.AP.4 — Sequence the development of an argument.
- **Supplementary references:** None directly cited on CPALMS page.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15013)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner map an argument by identifying the central claim, each piece of evidence, and the explicit or implicit reasoning that links the evidence to the claim?
  - Misconception to detect: Stopping at "claim + evidence" without identifying the reasoning; treating reasoning as another piece of evidence; missing stacked claims where the whole argument rests on sub-claims.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer "council debate" scene where a character presents a written proposal — learner tracks the argument structure across three paragraphs, identifying each claim, the evidence for it, and the reasoning the author uses to connect them.
  - Learner evidence: Argument map showing claim(s), evidence, and reasoning for each piece; brief explanation of how the reasoning connects evidence back to the claim.

## Standard 3: Reading — Reading Across Genres (ELA.5.R.3)

**Strand:** Reading
**Standard Grouping:** Reading Across Genres
**Purpose:** Students apply reading skills across genres with increasing analytical rigor. They analyze how figurative language contributes to meaning across text types, summarize with accuracy, and — at Grade 5 — compare and contrast primary and secondary sources on the same topic (a significant step up from comparing two authors of the same type).

---

### Benchmark: ELA.5.R.3.1

- **Code:** ELA.5.R.3.1
- **Strand:** Reading
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.R.3 — Reading Across Genres
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Analyze how figurative language contributes to meaning in text(s).
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS page.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Grade 5 builds on Grade 4's "explain how figurative language contributes to meaning" by adding "analyze" as the cognitive demand — students examine the relationship between the device and the meaning it creates, considering how the text would read differently without it and what specific effect the author achieves by choosing that particular device. Applicable across both literary and informational texts.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: identifying the device but not its contribution; saying a metaphor "makes it more interesting" without specifying the meaning it generates; not extending analysis to informational texts, where figurative language also appears.)
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends ELA.4.R.3.1 (explain how figurative language contributes to meaning); Grade 5 elevates to analysis and connects to R.1.4's work on poetic elements.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Five (5010046)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 5 (7710016)
  - Library Skills/Information Literacy Grade 5 (5011050)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.R.3.AP.1 — Identify examples of when figurative language is used to contribute to meaning in text(s).
- **Supplementary references:**
  - **Elementary Figurative Language (Appendix D):** Directly informs this benchmark. See `cpalms_supplementary_resources.md`, Resource 5 for content. Source URL: https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixd.pdf
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15014)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner identify a specific figurative device in a passage, explain the precise meaning it creates, and articulate how the text's meaning would change without it?
  - Misconception to detect: Labeling the device correctly but only paraphrasing it ("the simile means she was very tired") rather than analyzing its effect ("the simile places the character's exhaustion on a physical, visible scale that the reader can feel, not just understand").
  - Narrative/problem contexts: A Primer story uses personification to describe a failing machine — learner analyzes how the personification transfers emotional stakes from the mechanical problem to the human characters' situation.
  - Learner evidence: Correctly identifies the device; explains the specific meaning it constructs; offers a contrast ("without this device, the reader would only know X, but with it, we also understand Y").

### Benchmark: ELA.5.C.2.1

- **Code:** ELA.5.C.2.1

### Benchmark: ELA.5.V.1.2

- **Code:** ELA.5.V.1.2

- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.V.1 — Finding Meaning

## Standard 10: Foundational Skills — Learning and Applying Foundational Reading Skills (ELA.5.F.1)

**Strand:** Foundational Skills
**Standard Grouping:** Learning and Applying Foundational Reading Skills
**Purpose:** Students at Grade 5 apply broad phonics, word-analysis, and fluency skills. ELA.5.F.1.1 and ELA.5.F.1.2 are not present at Grade 5 — those benchmarks address print concepts and phonological awareness, which are mastered by the end of Grade 2. Grade 5 F.1.3 focuses on comprehensive phonics mastery (letter-sound correspondences, syllabication, and morphology), while F.1.4 focuses on fluency with prosody at Grade 5 text complexity.

---

### Benchmark: ELA.5.F.1.3

- **Code:** ELA.5.F.1.3
- **Strand:** Foundational Skills
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.F.1 — Learning and Applying Foundational Reading Skills
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Use knowledge of grade-appropriate phonics and word-analysis skills to decode words. Apply knowledge of all letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns, and morphology to read and write unfamiliar single-syllable and multisyllabic words in and out of context.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:** Not explicitly stated on CPALMS PrintStandard page.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Grade 5 F.1.3 represents near-complete foundational reading mastery: students apply the full system of letter-sound correspondences (including complex vowel patterns and consonant clusters), all syllabication pattern types, and morphological knowledge (roots, affixes) as an integrated decoding strategy — in and out of connected text context.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: defaulting to letter-by-letter decoding for long words instead of chunk-based decoding; not recognizing how morphology-based decoding differs from phonics-based decoding; confusing similar phoneme patterns.)
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Extends Grades 3–4 F.1.3 (decoding with phonics and morphology); Grade 5 is the capstone of the K–5 decoding progression.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Five (5010046)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 5 (7710016)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - Functional Reading Skills 3–5 (5010026)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.F.1.AP.3a — Apply knowledge of letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns and morphology to read and form familiar single-syllable and multisyllabic words in context.
- **Supplementary references:**
  - **Common Greek and Latin Roots 3–5 and Affixes:** Morphological knowledge (roots and affixes) is the primary decoding strategy at Grade 5 for multisyllabic words. See `cpalms_supplementary_resources.md`, Resources 7 and 8.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15004)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner correctly decode an unfamiliar multisyllabic story word by identifying syllable boundaries, relevant morphemes (roots/affixes), and letter-sound correspondences — both in connected text and when presented in isolation?
  - Misconception to detect: Sounding out every letter without recognizing morpheme chunks; failing to use context to confirm a decoded meaning; applying syllabication rules inconsistently.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer "word unlock" mechanic — when a highlighted multisyllabic word appears in the story text, learner taps it and is prompted to break it into syllables and morphemes to decode meaning, then use it in a sentence.
  - Learner evidence: Correct syllabication with accurate morpheme boundaries; accurate identification of any root and affix present; correct pronunciation/usage in a following sentence.

### Benchmark: ELA.5.F.1.4

- **Code:** ELA.5.F.1.4
- **Strand:** Foundational Skills
- **Standard grouping:** ELA.5.F.1 — Learning and Applying Foundational Reading Skills
- **Grade:** 5
- **Official description:** Read grade-level texts with accuracy, automaticity, and appropriate prosody or expression.
- **Official examples:** None explicitly stated.
- **Clarifications:**
  - Clarification 1: See Fluency Norms for grade-level norms. Norms are expressed as words correct per minute (WCPM), a measure that combines accuracy with rate.
  - Clarification 2: Appropriate prosody refers to pausing patterns during oral reading that reflect the punctuation and meaning of a text. See Sample Oral Reading Fluency Rubrics for prosody.
  - Clarification 3: Grade-level texts, for the purposes of fluency, are those within the grade band on quantitative text complexity measures and appropriate in content and qualitative measures.
- **Purpose and instructional strategies:** (Inferred) Students read Grade 5 texts (740L–1010L Lexile band) with all three fluency dimensions: accuracy (no decoding errors), automaticity (reading without effortful decoding), and prosody (phrasing, pausing, expression that reflects meaning). Repeated reading, reader's theater, and audio-assisted reading are common instructional approaches.
- **Common misconceptions or errors:** Not explicitly stated. (Inferred: conflating fast reading with fluent reading; ignoring complex punctuation cues for prosody; accurate but expressionless reading that signals inadequate prosodic development.)
- **Glossary terms:** Not explicitly stated.
- **Vertical alignment:** Continuous from Grade 3 F.1.4; Grade 5 applies to higher text complexity (740L–1010L vs. 420L–820L at Grade 3) with the same three-component fluency expectation.
- **Related courses or access points:**
  - Language Arts - Grade Five (5010046)
  - Access Language Arts - Grade 5 (7710016)
  - Basic Skills in Reading 3–5 (5010024)
  - Functional Reading Skills 3–5 (5010026)
  - English for Speakers of Other Languages Grade 5 (5010016)
  - **Access Point:** ELA.5.F.1.AP.4 — Read grade-level texts, at the student's ability level, with accuracy and expression using the student's mode of communication.
- **Supplementary references:**
  - **Fluency Norms (Appendix E — Hasbrouck-Tindal Chart):** Referenced by Clarification 1. Grade 5 WCPM norms by percentile and time of year:

    | Percentile | Fall WCPM | Winter WCPM | Spring WCPM |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 90th | 166 | 182 | 194 |
    | 75th | 139 | 156 | 168 |
    | 50th | 110 | 127 | 139 |
    | 25th | 85 | 99 | 109 |
    | 10th | 61 | 74 | 83 |

    Source: Hasbrouck & Tindal (2017). See `cpalms_supplementary_resources.md`, Resource 2. Source URL: https://cpalmsmediaprod.blob.core.windows.net/uploads/docs/standards/best/la/appendixe.pdf
  - **Sample Oral Reading Fluency Rubrics:** Referenced by Clarification 2. The NAEP Fluency Scale (Levels 1–4) and Multidimensional Fluency Scale (Expression & Volume, Phrasing, Smoothness, Pace) are used to evaluate prosody beyond WCPM rate. See `cpalms_supplementary_resources.md`, Resource 2.
- **Verification status:** Fully verified (https://www.cpalms.org/PreviewStandard/PrintStandard/15005)
- **Primer incorporation notes:**
  - Skill observed: Can the learner read a Grade 5 Primer passage (740L–1010L) aloud with accuracy (no decoding errors), at an appropriate rate (~110–139 WCPM at 50th percentile), and with prosody (pausing at commas and sentence breaks, varying expression for different sentence types)?
  - Misconception to detect: Confusing fast reading with prosodic reading; monotone delivery at an accurate rate without expression; halting word-by-word reading of grade-level text even with accurate decoding.
  - Narrative/problem contexts: Primer "read-aloud" feature records the learner reading a Grade 5 passage; AI or teacher reviews for three fluency dimensions using the NAEP rubric criteria.
  - Learner evidence: Audio recording at ~110+ WCPM (50th percentile baseline); accurate decoding across the passage; prosodic features present (sentence-final falling intonation, appropriate pause at semicolons/commas, expression variation for questions vs. statements).
