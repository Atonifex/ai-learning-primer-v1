## What strong math teaching does (grades 2–8)

The National Research Council's *Adding It Up* (2001) treats math proficiency as five strands that have to grow together, not as a ladder of drills then a later "understanding" unit: conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, strategic competence (forming and solving problems), adaptive reasoning (explaining why), and a disposition to see math as sensible and worth the effort. [National Academies, Adding It Up, chapter 4](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9822/chapter/6).

Two Institute of Education Sciences practice guides are the closest federal summaries for this age band:

- **Grades 4–8 problem solving** ([IES, 2012, revised 2018](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/16/Published)): prepare real problems; help students watch and reflect on their own solving; teach visual representations; show more than one strategy; connect the work to the concept and the notation.
- **Elementary intervention** ([IES, 2021](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/26)): systematic teaching of one idea; precise math language the child also uses; concrete and drawn representations; the number line; word problems taught on purpose; short timed work as one fluency tool, not the whole lesson.

For Primer that means a math sitting on one idea (equal groups of biscuits) should include a picture or objects, a chance to guess before being told, feedback that says what happened to the quantity, a second case that is not a copy, and a sentence or plan the child can defend. Fluency practice belongs inside that idea. It does not replace the reason.

## What other products do

These are patterns, not a ranking. Evidence quality varies. Vendor studies and correlations are weaker than a preregistered trial on a state test.

| Approach | What the child actually does | What holds up | What is weaker |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual puzzle path** (ST Math) | Games that show the quantity. Language is light. Advance after most puzzles at a level are right. | A large Southern California trial of grades 3–5 found higher California Standards Test math scores for schools using ST Math, strongest in grades 4–5. [MIND Research write-up of the IES-funded trial](https://info.mindresearch.org/hubfs/pdfs/research-results/ST-Math_RCT_2021_MR4.pdf). WWC has also recorded positive number and operations findings for an earlier ST Math study. | Easy to finish puzzles without saying why. Primer's captains need to speak the reason, because the camp has to use the plan. |
| **Adaptive lessons** (DreamBox) | Game lessons that branch on the child's moves. | WWC (2013): one kindergarten–grade 1 trial, potentially positive, small evidence base. [WWC DreamBox report](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/InterventionReports/wwc_dreambox_121013.pdf). | Old and narrow. Do not treat it as proof for grades 3–8. |
| **Lesson plus digital practice** (Zearn) | A classroom lesson and software on the same idea. | Zearn's large Texas grades 3–5 trial: about +0.11 SD on MAP over two years; the state test (STAAR) gain was positive and not statistically significant. [Zearn research summary](https://about.zearn.org/research). | The gain showed up when the digital work matched a real lesson, not as a side game. Usage studies that count only heavy users are easier to overread. |
| **Mastery videos and skills** (Khan Academy) | Watch, practice a skill to a bar, then the next skill. | A grades 3–8 program that coached teachers to get students about 35 minutes a week of Khan practice reported about 0.12–0.22 SD on some math measures. A review of that same trial says the prespecified state-test outcome was not significant (about 0.03 SD). [No-Spin Evidence Review](https://nospin.evidencebasedpolicy.org/articles/khoaching-khan-academy-support-grade-3-8-math-learning). Khan's own later write-ups emphasize dosage. | Mastery bars without a reason to care become a worksheet with a progress ring. Time-on-site is not the same as a state-test gain. |
| **Skill checklist** (IXL) | Many items on a chosen skill, instant right/wrong, a score. | An IXL-commissioned study, reviewed by SRI, associated IXL schools with about 3.6 percentage points higher Oklahoma proficiency (school-level, grades 3–8, three years). [SRI review](https://www.sri.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/QED-IXL-Math-11122021.pdf). | That is school-level and quasi-experimental. The child experience is a pile of items. It is the shape Primer's five short quizzes already risk. |
| **RPG costume** (Prodigy) | Walk a game; answer a multiple-choice item to cast a spell. | A Johns Hopkins-linked usage study found a tiny correlation: on the order of 888 questions for a one-point test gain, and more use did not keep helping. [Prodigy evaluation summary](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Prodigy-Evaluation-Key-Findings.pdf). A dissertation comparing grade 3 users and non-users found no significant Star Math difference. [Walden dissertation](https://scholarworks.waldenu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=14501&context=dissertations). | The game can be fun while the math stays a detached question. That is the failure mode to avoid. |

Amplify, the pattern already in §1.2, is a curriculum: one phenomenon, several investigations, evidence, then the student explains. It is not a points game. Primer's island should play that role for a solo child. Rho is the guide. The camp is the reason the explanation matters.

## Kumon

Kumon is a supplemental worksheet sequence, not a grade-level textbook. Official method: start where the child can succeed, ignore age, tiny steps, self-study, advance only with high accuracy and speed, instructor watches and adjusts rather than lecturing. [Kumon method](https://www.kumon.com/about-kumon/kumon-method).

What is worth keeping:

- Do not assume the onboarding grade is the child's math level. §4.4 already says this.
- One small step, then the next, only after the current step is solid.
- The child does the work. Rho does not answer it.
- Daily return to the same idea beats a tour of four subjects.
- A comfortable start makes a hard idea possible later.

What not to copy:

- Thousands of bare arithmetic items before a child ever says why. A parent who counted the early levels described on the order of 21,000 straight arithmetic problems across the nominal grades 1–4 worksheets, and only a thin set of word problems before fractions. [A parent's worksheet count](http://www.donaldsauter.com/kumon.htm). That count is one observer's tally, not Kumon's brochure.
- Speed gates as the definition of mastery. Fluency matters. A timer as the only door makes math a race.
- Staying on the same sheet for months when the child is accurate but slow, or stuck with no new representation.
- WWC's 2009 Kumon Math review found no studies that met its standards, so it drew no conclusion. [WWC Kumon, March 2009](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/InterventionReports/wwc_kumon_031009.pdf). That report is old. It is not a verdict that Kumon fails.
- A Bangladesh RCT in BRAC schools found very large gains on Kumon diagnostic scores per minute (about 1.5–2.6 SD) and smaller gains on a self-learning proficiency test (about 0.8–1.2 SD) after eight months. [JICA-RI Working Paper 156](https://www.jica.go.jp/Resource/english/our_work/evaluation/tech_and_grant/impact/c8h0vm00006eh46d-att/JICA-RI_WP_No.156.pdf). Those tests match the method's own speed-and-accuracy goal. They are not Florida state-test results, and the setting is not a U.S. homeschool.

Ivan's read — respect the sequence, add the reason to care — matches the split above. Kumon supplies the ladder. The island supplies the job the ladder is for. Neither is the whole sitting.

## Ivan's decisions — 2026-10-05

These are locked in MASTER §4.4. They are not a guess from this research note.

- A new idea is shown first, visually, as an example to copy. Then the child does the next one.
- A drawing tool is later. Not this slice.
- A diagnostic comes before assigned work. Otherwise the work is too easy and dull.
- One diagnostic per subject. Math is the first. The stop-rules are the engine the other subjects will reuse.
- The check finds a starting point. It is not a mastery score.

## What this suggests for the math dive, before any build

A Grade 3 equal-groups sitting (biscuits, `MA.3.NSO.2.2`, already in the bank) would:

1. Stay on that one idea for the sitting.
2. Start at a step the child can do, even if that is below the grade label.
3. Show the groups, not only the numerals.
4. Ask for a prediction, then feedback on that prediction.
5. Give a second case that changes the numbers.
6. End with a ration plan the camp keeps, in the child's words.
7. Keep a few fluent facts in the same idea. Do not open a second subject to manufacture variety.

Nothing here authorizes new standard codes or a change to the mastery formula.
