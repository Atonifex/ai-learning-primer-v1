# Primer MVP - Interaction Engines, Onboarding, and Core Gameplay Plan

## Vision

Primer is an interactive narrative learning experience for Grade 3 learners that blends:
- Story-driven adventure
- Educational mini-games
- Persistent progression
- AI-assisted tutoring
- Emotional continuity
- Visual camp-building progression

The product should feel like:
> An animated interactive graphic novel with tactile learning systems.

NOT:
- a worksheet platform
- a chatbot
- an open-world game
- a traditional LMS

The core emotional fantasy is:
> "You are the captain of a stranded expedition trying to survive, rebuild, and eventually reconnect with the Guild."

The educational mechanics are embedded inside survival, exploration, communication, and rebuilding.

---

# Core MVP Principles

## 1. Story First
The story is the emotional engine.

Every session begins with:
- narrative setup
- dialogue
- tension/problem
- mission framing

Then educational activities are introduced naturally within the story.

Example:
NOT:
> Solve 10 multiplication problems.

BUT:
> We need to divide lumber evenly before the tide comes in.

---

## 2. Reusable Interaction Engines
The MVP should focus on a small number of reusable systems.

The AI orchestrator chooses:
- which engine to launch
- the educational standard
- difficulty
- story context
- rewards

The engines themselves remain stable.

This prevents content explosion and allows scalable educational generation.

---

## 3. Persistent Camp Progression
The player has a persistent home base.

The camp visually evolves over time.

The camp begins as:
- a sleeping bag
- scattered wreckage
- driftwood
- a small fire

The camp gradually upgrades into:
- tents
- storage
- water systems
- workshops
- docks
- towers
- classrooms/labs
- Guild communication systems

The village is the emotional anchor of progression.

---

## 4. Emotional Tone
The tone should follow:

## Balanced Pixar-Style Adventure

The world includes:
- tension
- danger
- mystery
- humor
- warmth
- optimism
- friendship

The tone should never become traumatic or emotionally overwhelming.

Rho provides:
- warmth
- comic relief
- emotional grounding
- curiosity

---

## Scene 1 - Cinematic Introduction

Animated comic-panel style sequence.

Visuals:
- storm
- crashing ship
- broken mast
- dark ocean
- lightning
- shouting crew
- mysterious island silhouette

Minimal text.

Strong music and atmosphere later.

The learner sees:
- the expedition ship
- Guild symbols
- cargo
- maps
- crew members
- chaos during the wreck

The final shot:
- black screen
- ocean sounds
- fire crackling

Then:

Rho:
> "Captain...? Captain, wake up."

This is the emotional beginning of the entire product.

---

## Scene 2 - Naming the Captain

Rho asks:
> "Can you still remember your name?"

The learner enters:
- display name

This immediately embeds the learner inside the story.

---

## Scene 3 - First Emotional Hook

The learner discovers:
- the ship is destroyed
- some crew members are missing

- communication with the Guild is impossible

The learner receives:
- responsibility
- urgency

> The captain of the stranded expedition.

## Scene 4 - First Camp Reveal

## Scene 5 - First Mission

# Core MVP Interaction Engines

The MVP focuses on 3 major reusable systems.

---

# Engine 1 - Dialogue Engine

## Purpose
Primary narrative interaction system.

Supports:
- ELA
- social studies
- emotional engagement
- perspective taking
- writing
- roleplaying
- decision-making

---

## Core Gameplay

The learner:
- talks with characters
- chooses responses
- negotiates
- persuades
- interprets perspectives
- asks questions
- writes letters/reports
- makes decisions

---

## Types of Dialogue Activities

### 1. Branching Conversations

Example:
A crew member wants more food than others.

The learner chooses how to respond.

Choices can affect:
- morale
- trust
- future dialogue
- Guild reputation

---

### 2. Perspective Challenges

The learner explains:
- why two characters disagree
- what each character wants
- which solution is fairest

Supports:
- empathy
- perspective standards
- evidence-based reasoning

---

### 3. Guild Letters

One of the most important systems.

The learner writes:
- updates
- reports
- requests
- explanations
- scientific findings
- expedition summaries

The Guild responds later.

Better communication yields:
- supplies
- reputation
- upgrades
- support

This system integrates:
- writing
- summarization
- evidence
- narrative
- AI evaluation
- progression rewards

---

### 4. Diaries and Journal Reading

The learner discovers:
- old journals
- crew notes
- maps
- damaged logs
- messages

These become:
- reading comprehension activities
- mystery clues
- emotional storytelling devices

---

## AI Companion

The AI robot companion acts as:
- tutor
- helper
- explainer
- conversational support

This companion is:
- flexible
- question-answering
- encouraging
- educational

However:
- major narrative progression remains handcrafted
- the AI companion does not control the main story

This prevents narrative chaos.

---

## Dialogue Engine UI

Core UI elements:
- animated character portraits
- speech bubbles
- selectable responses
- text-to-speech button
- journal UI
- letter-writing interface
- relationship indicators later

---

# Engine 2 - Investigation Lab

## Purpose
Primary science and reasoning system.

Supports:
- science
- evidence
- classification
- observation
- experimentation
- logical reasoning
- data interpretation

---

## Core Gameplay

The learner:
- investigates problems
- observes evidence
- categorizes objects
- performs lightweight experiments
- writes conclusions
- explains findings

---

## Types of Investigation Activities

### 1. Categorization

Examples:
- safe vs unsafe water
- edible vs poisonous plants
- damaged vs usable supplies
- metal vs wood vs cloth materials

Mechanics:
- drag and drop
- sorting
- grouping
- classification

Highly reusable.

---

### 2. Evidence Analysis

The learner examines:
- footprints
- weather clues
- broken structures
- animal traces
- damaged cargo

Then answers:
- what happened?
- what evidence supports it?
- what should the crew do next?

---

### 3. Lightweight Experiments

Examples:
- test which shelter material resists water
- determine which container holds the most water
- compare drying speed
- test stability of structures

The learner:
- predicts
- tests
- observes
- concludes

---

### 4. Investigation Reports

The learner writes:
- findings
- evidence summaries
- scientific explanations

These reports can later:
- improve Guild reputation
- unlock support
- become part of the captain's log

---

## Investigation Lab UI

Potential UI components:
- draggable objects
- evidence board
- notebook/journal
- observation cards
- prediction boxes
- animated reactions
- lightweight simulation visuals

---

# Engine 3 - Camp Progression Layer

## Purpose
Primary long-term motivation system.

This is NOT a sandbox builder.

The learner does NOT freely place buildings.

Instead:
- upgrades are guided
- progression is curated
- visuals evolve automatically

This massively reduces technical scope.

---

## Core Loop

The learner:
- completes missions
- earns resources
- upgrades camp structures
- unlocks new story opportunities
- visually improves the camp

---

## Core Resources

### Lumber
Used for:
- shelter
- storage
- walls
- docks

### Metal
Used for:
- tools
- repairs
- communication devices
- advanced upgrades

### Guild Reputation
Represents:
- trust
- expedition credibility
- support from home

Higher Guild reputation unlocks:
- supply drops
- advanced equipment
- communication systems
- larger missions

---

## Initial Camp States

### Stage 0
- sleeping bag
- weak fire
- broken crates
- empty beach

### Stage 1
- basic shelter
- cleaner camp
- supply pile
- drying racks

### Stage 2
- storage tent
- stronger firepit
- gathering area
- workbench

### Stage 3
- watchtower
- water system
- dock repair
- Guild communication platform

---

## Upgrade Structure

The learner selects:

Example:

[ Build Water Collector ]
Cost:
- 40 Lumber
- 10 Metal

Then:
- construction animation plays
- camp updates visually
- new activities unlock
- characters react

This provides:
- progression
- emotional payoff
- visible accomplishment

Without requiring complex building systems.

# Initial Mission Structure

## Mission 1 - The Wreck

Narrative Goal:
Assess what survived.

Educational Goals:
- categorization
- measurement
- observation
- basic writing

Activities:
- sort damaged supplies
- identify useful materials
- read damaged crew notes
- write first Guild update

Reward:
- lumber
- first camp cleanup
- improved firepit

---

## Mission 2 - Divide the Supplies

Narrative Goal:
Prevent conflict.

Educational Goals:
- fairness
- grouping
- fractions
- dialogue choices

Activities:
- distribute food fairly
- negotiate with crew members
- explain reasoning
- update captain's log

Reward:
- morale increase
- storage tent
- Guild reputation

This creates:
- consistency
- quality
- scalability
- emotional coherence
