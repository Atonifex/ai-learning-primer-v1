## Why shorten it

V04's ~4:47 is not necessary. Repeated leadership/reward explanation, a resource-definition aside, several separate island inserts, a corridor speech, long boarding/travel coverage and a future-plan montage delay the first playable action.

V05 targets **~3:10 immediate Yes**, **~3:24 two No replies**, plus untimed choice pauses. About97s shorter than V04 (~34%) without speeding up speech. These are paper estimates, not recorded timings. Exact opening language means this is unlikely to fit comfortably into a 90-second complete briefing/crash/objective movie; don't cram or remove the safety/mission logic to chase that limit.

## Final condensed screenplay

### 00:00–00:20 — Wilhelm welcomes the captain

First-person eye-level room shot. Sergeant Wilhelm, with his distinct mustache, sits upright behind the desk. Controlled friendly greeting; warm eyes and stern posture. No face on monitors, no captain gesture, no automatic camera change.

**WILHELM:** “Welcome to planet Maya, Captain. I'm Sergeant Wilhelm of Merchant Corp. I know you're coming to our planet for the first time, and your skills will come in handy.”

### 00:20–00:40 — Why Merchant Corp needs you

Wilhelm stands and steps toward a large **map on the wall**. Camera follows one modest purposeful movement, keeping him physical in the room. Mainland base and distant Fortuna appear on the map; labels are added later in editing, not generated.

**WILHELM:** “Here at Merchant Corp, we gather resources like lumber, oil, and other rare resources, then sell them to people on other planets. However, it's getting harder to find these resources. That's why we need you to help us.”

Only small grammar/pause correction from user's line: **on** other planets; short spoken sentences. No commodity lectures or separate cargo-port montage.

### 00:40–00:58 — Wall map becomes Fortuna

Wilhelm points to Fortuna's outline. Camera pushes toward that spot; his hand/arm leaves frame as the outline fills the picture. Match the coastline into a clean aerial island shot. River mouth, beach, flat plains, forest and inland ridge align with the map.

**WILHELM, V.O.:** “We've just found Fortuna. No team has explored it. It's far from our main base. It may have oil, gold, and useful wood. But storms and wild animals may make the trip dangerous.”

Allow a brief quiet beauty beat as the forest/river open into view. Then remote survey weather along the ridge and a partly hidden animal moving beneath canopy. These are uncertain sky observations, not a prior ground expedition or a confirmed attack. No settlement, wells, gold bars, hands or cockpit frame in island imagery. The map hand must disappear before the aerial transition completes.

### 00:58–01:12 — The job

Return to the physical Wilhelm beside the wall map. He turns toward captain, firm and clear.

**WILHELM:** “Explore Fortuna and report what you find. If the resources are there and the site is safe, we'll ask you to build a base and start collecting them.”

Survey/report comes first; resource presence remains unproven. Collecting follows confirmation.

### 01:12–01:27 — Your reward and choice

Wilhelm returns to the desk, sets down mission folder, and reaches the waiting anchor.

**WILHELM:** “You'll lead your own crew, build your own base, and get ten percent of the profit. That's the money left after costs. Will you take the mission?”

**APP:** Silent matching-endpoint loop, **Yes / No**, 10% share. Choices appear only after audio. No early thumbs-up; waiting is untimed. Sound/captions/Skip remain available outside the generated picture.

**First No, +7s — WILHELM:** “You want more? All right. Fifteen percent. Do we have a deal?”

Friendly reserve gives way to thought and a small concession; return to same waiting pose. **Yes / No**, 15%.

**Second No, +7s — WILHELM:** “Again? Twenty percent. That's my final offer.”

Visible controlled exasperation, short breath, firm eye contact; return to anchor. **Yes only**, 20%; Skip stays available. No threats or shaming. Accepted share saves before shared continuation.

**Optional comic timing inside these7s:** Wilhelm pauses, sets his pen down with one precise click, then delivers the final offer. A restrained low plucked-note accent can underline his deadpan look; no clown sound or exaggerated tantrum. The pause fits the line's existing timing.

### 01:27–01:40 — Deal and Rho

**WILHELM:** “We have a deal. Meet Rho, your first mate.”

Rho gives her introduction during the brief handshake and release; she looks toward the captain with clear, professional warmth. Allow a natural read within this 13-second beat; adjust the cut if needed rather than rush her line. One short captain thumbs-up after acceptance only. Rho enters through established door; firm short handshake and immediate release. Wilhelm stays behind. Normal colleague distance; no lingering gaze or head tilt.

**RHO:** “I'm Rho, your first mate. I'll help you learn about Maya and Fortuna, and guide you along the way.”

He gives Rho a professional nod and closes the mission folder. No added villain cue; later conflict can grow from his priorities and the captain's experiences.

### 01:40–01:55 — Departure

Brief match-on-action professional walk to hangar; Rho left, attention on destination. No corridor speech, hand-holding or romantic music.

**Optional quick visual beat:** a breeze flips one corner of Rho's paper checklist; she neatly pins it back with a mildly annoyed glance. Half a second, practical and composed. No paper chasing or flight delay; no extra dialogue. Use this or the negotiating reaction rather than stacking gags.

Exterior establishes one teal/cream/brass twin-prop survey aircraft, fixed wings, tail and wheels on mainland Maya runway. Cut to first-person cockpit: captain hands on controls, Rho strapped into left seat, blue escape handle fixed left. Aircraft accelerates on its wheels, rotates and lifts; same heading/design inside and outside. No hover or sudden second craft.

**RHO:** “Crew ready. The path is clear.”

Continuous engine bed carries across walk/boarding/runway coverage where appropriate; give the forward roll real screen time.

### 01:55–02:01 — Fortuna ahead

Clean exterior aircraft/landscape coverage. Establish same coast and ridge; storm moves quickly beyond ridge. Six seconds for place and scale. No dialogue, hands, people or cockpit borders in exterior shot.

### 02:01–02:08 — Try to turn back

First-person cockpit: rain arrives, cloud bank crosses route. Rho's brows tighten; lips firm, body braced within harness. No smile.

**RHO:** “That storm is moving fast. Turn back!”

Captain turns controls toward clear sea; exterior confirms retreat begins. Crew are not intentionally flying into a known dangerous storm.

### 02:08–02:16 — Lightning strike

One readable exterior lightning strike hits the aircraft near an engine. Localized flame appears at that engine; propeller falters. Cut inside: power indication drops, engine sound collapses, nose falls and aircraft begins a steep descent toward coast. No instant teleport to the beach or zero-height bailout.

**RHO:** “The engine's on fire! We're losing power!”

Keep fire localized outside the cabin. No engulfed people, burn injuries, gore, explosive fireball or repeated strobe-light flashes. This supersedes V04's gust-only engine failure; the strike is the clear cause of fire/power loss/descent.

### 02:16–02:26 — Captain gets the crew out

**RHO:** “Crew, get ready! Captain, pull the blue handle!”

Captain pulls established left handle. Fictional escape system operates through its independent emergency mechanism even as aircraft loses power. Exterior shows occupied seats moving clear of wings/propellers, then chutes opening while sufficient height remains. Define crew count, seats, canopy paths and motion in the reference sheet before generation; do not invent a real aircraft procedure.

### 02:26–02:36 — Safe escape, empty aircraft crash

All occupied chutes visibly clear the falling aircraft before impact. Storm wind spreads crew between beach and forest clearings. Empty aircraft drops/skids into shallows at the same beach, damaging wing/hull but leaving recognizable repairable wreck.

No occupied impact, death/injury or fireball. Exterior has no captain body parts. First-person parachute descent can briefly show its own straps after an explicit camera cut; cut before landing. Impact/wind fade to surf and passage of night.

### 02:36–02:43 — Dawn

Same wreck, beach and river mouth in calm dawn. No settlement. Captain view rises; Rho checks responsiveness at ordinary helping distance. Her concern eases only after she knows captain is safe.

**RHO:** “Captain. Are you all right?”

Captain gives a small first-person nod, not another thumbs-up. No romantic face close-up.

### 02:43–03:03 — The new objective

Rho looks from distant chutes/tree line to a dry camp clearing and wreck. Three motivated views, not a future civilization montage.

**RHO:** “The crew is scattered. We can't reach the base. You're still the captain. Find the crew. Build a camp. We'll rebuild the ship. I'll help you learn to lead.”

She offers a route slate for captain's plan.

**RHO:** “One day, you could lead a space empire. First, let's get your crew home.”

Empire remains a future ambition. No literal empire/city preview or unbuilt gameplay promise as current functionality. Do not claim all missing crew already found.

### 03:03–03:10 — First playable action

Supply crate at safe wreck; Rho steps aside, leaving captain space to act.

**Optional background comic beat after safety:** a small harmless beach crab briefly inspects the crate, then scuttles aside as captain approaches. Soft claw taps, no attack, jump scare or interruption of missing-crew concerns. Keep within existing shot; Rho's relief stays restrained.

**RHO:** “Start with the wreck. What food and tools can we save?”

End into existing name/beach/task onboarding. The crashed mission is scripted safely; no graded emergency responses. Learning-purpose app disclosure remains separate, as specified in V04/`docs/LEARNING_PURPOSE_AND_PROGRESSION.md`, with prototype-versus-future assessment wording accurate.
