# Primer creative improvement cycle

User preference accepted 2026-10-05. Applies to Primer creative work; this is project memory, not a global setting for other apps.

When Ivan says “iterate upon this,” “reflect and improve,” “do an improvement cycle,” or similar, default to **two critique-and-revision rounds** unless he specifies another scope. The goal is compelling, clear, emotionally engaging writing for Grades 3–8, not merely factual completeness.

Read `PROJECT_MEMORY.md`, the relevant current artifact and accepted creative constraints first. Update that memory concisely after meaningful discoveries; retain versions, asset locations and unresolved acceptance points without secrets.

1. Review the current artifact from three clearly labeled lenses: screenwriter (desire, agency, cause, stakes and emotion); director (point of view, acting, continuity, visual storytelling and sound); producer/editor (child comprehension, pacing, practical execution, reuse and cost). These may be simulated perspectives; never imply actual filmmakers were consulted. Use agents only when separately authorized.
2. Identify concrete weaknesses and changes. Preserve accepted premise/style and distinguish proposals from decisions. Write revision 1, rather than stopping at advice.
3. Critique revision 1 through the same lenses. Seek remaining weaknesses and tradeoffs; do not merely endorse the draft. Write revision 2 incorporating justified changes and include the usable script in the response.
4. Check the causal chain, player role, simple spoken language, emotion progression, natural transitions, age suitability, resource/setting continuity, and honest learning purpose. Include Ivan's occasional fun/comedic-effects preference (2026-10-05): dry reactions, brief visual surprises and restrained playful sound cues where appropriate; no gag during lightning/fire/escape or false positivity in danger. Humor should fit existing beat time and preserve competence. Let dialogue and dramatic beats determine timing; generation chunk lengths must not dictate narrative structure.
5. Separate editorial checks from verified production checks. Transcript matching is not pronunciation, voice identity, lip-sync or listening verification. A paper edit is not a measured read-through. Keep all audio captions editable.
6. Preserve footage and source versions as reusable library assets with limitations. Critique/rewrite requests do not automatically authorize paid generation, purchases, publishing or app implementation. State what was improved, what was checked, and what still needs actual playback/acceptance.
