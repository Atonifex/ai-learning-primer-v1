## What the first cut taught us

Ivan reports that the officer says “corprotation” instead of “corporation,” and that the fixed five-second scenes feel disconnected. Treat this as playback feedback, not an independently verified listening result. The six original clips and all edit/audio/caption sources remain in `public/cinematics/prologue-v1/`; see `LIBRARY.md`. The first edit is an editorial experiment, not release-approved.

The problem is structure as well as execution: each clip introduces a fact and resets speaker, location or intention. Equal durations turn the storyboard into the screenplay. The captain accepts before understanding the mission. Three officer appearances repeat the briefing without deepening it. Rho arrives as another fact rather than as a relationship. The ending offers no dramatic handoff to the storm.

These are three simulated professional lenses, not feedback obtained from real filmmakers or endorsements by named people. Their comments are creative judgments, not measured audience results.

## Revision 1: coherent but still too informational

Draft dialogue, with continuous briefing audio and flexible cuts:

- **Officer:** “Captain, other planets need oil, gold, and silver. Your crew will search this island for them.”
- **Narrator, over connected cargo inserts:** “Trade carries materials between planets, helping people build homes, ship food, and travel.”
- **Officer, returning to the same communicator:** “Find what is there and send us your report. The Merchant Corporation can extract those resources, sell them, and earn money. Will you take the mission?”
- **Captain:** Nonverbal thumbs-up, after the request.
- **Rho, beside the wheel:** “I'm Rho, your first mate. Let's get your crew to that island.”
- **Transition:** Clouds gather over the destination as the ship approaches.

This version fixes causal order and acceptance timing, but does not yet earn production approval.

## Revision 2: proposed screenplay

Working title: **A mission worth taking**. Approximately 30–35 seconds; below is a 34-second paper edit, not measured performance timing. About 70 spoken words. Read aloud with space for acting before locking the duration. Do not accelerate speech to meet an arbitrary slot. Shot cuts and generated clip lengths are separate decisions.

### 0–4 seconds — The invitation

First-person in the scout airship cockpit. Only captain's hands/navy cuffs are visible. The professionally positive officer appears on the communicator, meeting the captain's gaze. A quiet engine hum establishes one continuous location.

**OFFICER:** “Captain, we've got a mission for your crew.”

She brings a briefing display into view. No early thumbs-up.

### 4–10 seconds — Why the mission matters

The briefing display fills the frame. One marked cargo crate moves through an inhabited off-island port. Match its movement into a loading bay; short glimpses of home construction, food cargo and a departing civilian transport give the words concrete meaning. These are connected illustrative inserts, not buildings on the surveyed island. Show materials safely sealed; do not imply gold directly builds houses or that the cargo already came from this island.

**NARRATOR:** “Across the planets, trade brings materials for homes, food shipments, and travel.”

One continuous narrator sentence over these images. Retain the cockpit sound bed beneath it. The same briefing display transitions to the island map rather than jumping to an unrelated scene.

### 10–23 seconds — The job and the choice

The officer remains in the same communicator while a plain island outline and resource illustrations appear beside her. Oil container and gold/silver ore samples are possible survey targets, not discoveries. The camera gently approaches the map; warm confident delivery, with emphasis on **find**. Show the actual uninhabited coastline through the cockpit glass: beach, river plain, hills, forest and inland mountains.

**OFFICER:** “This uninhabited island may hold oil, gold, and silver. Find what's there. The Merchant Corporation can extract it, sell it, and earn money. Will you take the mission?”

The commercial purpose is explicit. The officer asks; a brief beat lets the viewer understand the decision. The captain raises a thumbs-up. The officer gives a small approving nod. This acceptance must occur **after** the question, even if that requires a new picture take later.

### 23–30 seconds — The partner

Follow the captain's hand back to the wheel. Rho stands beside the captain, consistent with approved identity and costume. She glances from the accepted assignment to the destination, then warmly toward the viewer. She is capable and supportive; the captain remains in charge.

**RHO:** “I'm Rho, your first mate. You steer. I'll find the way.”

Leave a little time for the hand settling on the wheel and Rho's warm expression. No rear-view captain.

### 30–34 seconds — The turn

The island draws closer. A dark cloud bank partly covers the inland ridge. A gust rattles a loose chart edge; the engine hum continues. Rho's smile falls. Her eyes move from the captain to the windshield; she braces at the rail. Concern is clear without horror, flashing effects or injury.

**RHO, quieter and alert:** “Captain… look ahead.”

Stay in the same first-person viewpoint as weather builds. Carry the wind into the next storm sequence. Do not crash in the prologue. The final emotional move is trust → possibility → unease, leaving a reason to watch the next scene.
