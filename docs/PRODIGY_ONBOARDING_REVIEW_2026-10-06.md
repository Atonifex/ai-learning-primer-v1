# Prodigy onboarding review and Primer learning priorities

Date: 2026-10-06. Requested by Ivan after playing Prodigy Math's introduction. Analysis and proposed next work only; no app implementation or paid generation.

## Product clarification during this review — supersedes the initial slice recommendation

**Accepted user direction, 2026-10-06:** Primer is a **guided learning experience to help learners become their best selves**. Do not describe the product to learners or parents as a game. Understanding how the world works, asking questions and making discoveries should be the primary reason to continue. Rebuilding a society is the long-term inquiry context. Ivan finds counting crates a weak opening hook; he proposes investigating the crash and rebuilding communication with the mainland. The exact opening phenomenon remains undecided.

The next development priority is a strong **authored stock experience**, adapted to grade and subject, before expanding generated learning content. Make asking questions easy through suggested question bubbles, voice and the learner's own wording. Personal interests can shape investigations and branches. These are user-requested capabilities; the particular implementation below is proposed, not shipped.

**Revised recommendation:** author one compelling anchor investigation first, then implement its supported learning loop. The equal-groups episode later in this document is an illustration of feedback and application mechanics; it no longer represents the recommended opening or organizing curiosity. Making crate-counting practice more elaborate would not satisfy this clarification.

### Candidate anchor and the crash question

V05 explicitly shows lightning causing engine fire and power loss. “Why did we crash?” could support explanation of the mechanism, comparison of hypotheses or later safety/design decisions. As an opening mystery it has limited force when the cinematic has already revealed its cause. Do not invent sabotage or a hidden culprit to repair this weakness.

**Recommended candidate, not accepted canon:** “We can hear a distant signal. Why can't they hear our reply?” The observable phenomenon is the asymmetry between receiving and reaching someone else; the engineering problem is getting a useful message to the mainland. The author must define plausible equipment, distances, available evidence and the actual cause before using this premise. Neither a broken radio nor these signal observations currently exists in the app merely because it appears here.

This can sustain questions about energy, devices, distance, location, the information a message needs, and how people coordinate to build and maintain infrastructure. Do not answer all of them on entry. Let the learner notice, predict and choose a small investigation that changes what they know. “Rebuild society” remains the arc; one concrete observation starts today's work.

### Proposed inquiry structure

1. **Notice:** show a short, concrete puzzling event or conflicting pieces of evidence.
2. **Wonder:** offer two or three relevant questions plus “Ask my own” and voice. A selected bubble is an invitation to investigate, not permission for a long Rho lecture.
3. **Predict:** capture a tentative explanation or choice and its reason without making uncertain thinking feel like failure.
4. **Investigate:** use an authored observation, measurement, source or comparison, with scaffolding appropriate to the learner's starting point.
5. **Explain and revise:** Rho asks what the evidence supports, supplies needed instruction, and helps the learner correct their account.
6. **Apply:** make a communication/camp decision using that understanding; the learner's actual work persists.
7. **Check and revisit:** use a fresh case and later recall, with guided and independent evidence distinguished.

Example entry questions: “What could stop a message getting through?”, “How could we test our idea?”, “What does the person receiving it need to know?” These are authored suggestions, not assessed correct answers or a fabricated live scene.

Branch curiosity in bounded steps: save the current investigation, offer a short optional branch, connect its result to the anchor, and let the captain return or deliberately change focus. Rho should neither block every diversion nor follow endless tangents that remove opportunities to practice, explain and apply. Retain one chosen subject per sitting; connect other subject lenses across sittings rather than demanding all subjects in one session.

### Grade, interest and orchestration

Share the anchor situation across grades while varying representations, prerequisite instruction, task complexity, independence and explanation criteria. Easier wording alone is not grade adaptation. Grade 3 might interpret simple quantities or choose essential message information; later levels might compare data, evaluate a test or reason about technical tradeoffs. These are activity-design examples, not verified Florida grade mappings. Grade/subject authoring must use the seeded authoritative catalogs; Grade 5–8 require curriculum work before supported experiences can be claimed.

Use expressed interests to select a meaningful question or analogy within the same learning objective: building → how a structure helps a signal; nature → what the terrain evidence says; storytelling → what makes an urgent message understandable. Do not permanently label a child from a single choice. Preserve the same intellectual requirement and ask whether the interest treatment improved their understanding, not just engagement.

The centralized orchestrator should know the anchor question, current learner question/hypothesis, selected subject, supporting evidence, help used, unresolved idea and next authored action. Rho invokes tools to show evidence, open work and save results. Generation can later vary context and extend approved branches; it should not invent curricular alignment or replace a missing teaching sequence. First prove a curated unit with dependable wrong-answer support, fresh application and later recall.

## What the evidence supports

Prodigy offers a clear fantasy identity, guide relationship, stable presentation, legible targets, immediate game consequences and an obvious next destination. Those are useful design accomplishments even if this introduction feels academically thin.

The strongest concern is the **causal connection between subject knowledge and play**. In the pictured flow, answering a geometry question restores combat energy. A different academic question could replace it without changing the combat decision. The learner can become better at progressing through the game while the game asks little about how they think.

Difficulty, format and feedback need separate judgments. A simple warm-up can teach controls or reduce anxiety. Two choices can support a demanding reasoning task. Numeric entry does not automatically demand understanding. A drawing pad is valuable only if the task and feedback use the representation. The weakness is persistent low challenge or a shallow attempt→answer-reveal path, not the existence of easy items or multiple choice.

Keep the efficacy conclusion narrower than the UX verdict. Prodigy's [parent documentation](https://prodigygame.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005952803-Getting-Started-with-Prodigy-Math-Parent-FAQ) describes initial placement and subsequent adaptation. This may explain early easy items, but does not verify what happened in Ivan's account. A [2020 Johns Hopkins evaluation](https://jscholarship.library.jhu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/c38fbad0-2746-4c00-8654-5d0854d2a99c/content) found positive associations between usage and achievement gains in a correlational case-study design. It also recommended limiting time in non-content activities. Neither that study nor these screenshots establish a causal effect of today's onboarding.

## Primer's advantage and exposure

**Foundations already present:** an explicit learning purpose; one chapter problem; subject choice; saved starting checks; worked examples; specific mathematical feedback; persistent camp and learning state; and a next-action board. MASTER §4.16 already specifies a focused dive followed by learner work the camp uses. This review reinforces that direction.

**Current exposure:** the complete dive is not implemented. `MathPractice.tsx` progresses example→question→feedback and stops; it has no correction or fresh follow-up after that feedback. `mathPractice.ts` supplies one different case per starting skill. The five-question checks are Grade 3 starter samples and show examples before answers, so they provide supported evidence rather than clean independent assessment. Grade 4 catalogs do not by themselves create Grade 4 teaching; Grade 5–8 coverage remains unresolved.

Dialogue currently fills the screen, with chat on the left and a large Rho portrait on the right on desktop. It obscures the world instead of preserving Prodigy's world-above/dialogue-below continuity. The underlying overworld is paused. A bottom scene dialogue treatment is a good candidate for guided story beats; rich learning tools still need their own readable space. This is a proposal, not a change to the current dialogue lock or an authorization to animate/generate new world content.

Camp upgrades are more coherent rewards than arbitrary stars, but **a tent granted after an unrelated quiz can still be a detached reward**. The next step is for a learner's actual result to determine a quantity, arrangement or decision the camp uses. Merely renaming currency as rations does not accomplish this.

Rho's value must appear in her response to the learner's difficulty: appropriate hint, representation, correction and support that fades. Generic encouragement or solving the task for the captain would preserve the same shallow loop with more dialogue.

## Learning-loop mechanics illustration — opening recommendation superseded above

After the anchor unit is authored/reviewed, its subject episodes should use the saved starting point and existing subject-focus flow. The following **Grade 3 math example** illustrates dependable practice, correction and application mechanics. It is not the proposed first phenomenon. Select a skill supported by the seeded catalog and authored content; equal groups must not be assigned to every captain.

1. Present one concrete camp problem and a short visual worked example when the learner needs it.
2. Let the captain solve a related problem through a bounded response: number, marked groups or brief spoken explanation. Do not require a generic writing box.
3. On an error, explain the relevant distinction and let the learner correct the work. Do not diagnose a misconception from one wrong click as certain.
4. Offer varied related attempts, fading help. Four to six is a starting design estimate, not a research-established quota. Move faster when readiness is clear; respond to struggle with useful support.
5. Present an unfamiliar application without hints or an adjacent worked solution. Keep supported and independent evidence distinct under the existing evidence system; do not redesign the mastery formula.
6. Save the actual mathematical result/arrangement as the camp plan, and show the camp using it. Generate the artifact from the task work; do not restore the rejected mandatory camp-sentence gate.
7. Revisit the concept in a later sitting, using a changed context and recording what help was needed.

Worked examples alternating with problem solving, explanation and spaced retrieval have support in the [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1). The specific episode, timing and progression thresholds above are product proposals. [Habgood and Ainsworth's 2011 study](https://tca2.education.illinois.edu/docs/librariesprovider23/default-document-library/j-of-the-learning-sc-2011-habgood.pdf) found greater learning in a game variant whose core mechanics used the mathematics than in a variant using questions between levels. That supports investigating integration; it does not establish Primer's effectiveness.

## Two editorial critique/revision rounds

These are **simulated screenwriter, director and producer/editor lenses**, applied locally. No external reviewers or sub-agents were consulted. This is a paper learning encounter, not filmed dialogue or validated curriculum.

**Initial candidate:** answer a biscuit question, receive rations and a camp upgrade.

**Round 1 critique:**
- Screenwriter: the captain wants supplies, but their reasoning has no distinct effect on the outcome.
- Director: show the supplies the child is considering, rather than describing them in chat and then revealing unrelated decoration.
- Producer/editor: keep the existing map; make one learning episode complete before adding another destination or collectible system.

**Revision 1:** the captain calculates equal bundles of supplies. The displayed arrangement and saved camp plan use the calculation. A miss gets a specific explanation and a correction opportunity.

**Round 2 critique:**
- Screenwriter: the captain should commit a plan and see it used; avoid Rho doing the reasoning and the child merely approving it.
- Director: distinguish the current bundles, the child's proposed count and the corrected arrangement. Keep instructions short, and the relevant objects visible.
- Producer/editor: correction on the same question can be copying. Add a changed case without help, and a later revisit. Save structured task work without another mandatory reflection form.

**Revision 2 — usable paper encounter, for a captain ready for equal groups:**

| Beat | Rho / task | Learner action and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Need | “We need to count the biscuits before we pack them.” | The task opens at Camp; no required sightseeing. |
| Model, if needed | Show two bundles with four biscuits in each. “Two bundles. Four in each. That's eight.” | Learner sees the groups and total together. |
| Attempt | Show five bundles of three. “How many biscuits altogether?” | Captain gives a count; 15 is the authored expected answer. |
| Error support | For answer 8: “Five is the number of bundles. Three is in each bundle. Count three in every bundle.” | Highlight each group. Learner revises; a hint-used flag distinguishes this from independent evidence. |
| Practice | Change bundle counts and representation, including an equation matching task if useful. | Fade prompts when successful. Preserve retries and work across reload. |
| Fresh application | Show three trays with six biscuits on each. “How many should we write on the supply label?” | Independent expected count 18; no worked example or hint beside this check. More support remains available, but then the evidence is supported. |
| Apply | “Let's label these trays with your count.” | Corrected task result creates an 18-biscuit label/plan; the camp visibly uses it. A mistake is safely revised, with no loss of supplies or crew harm. |
| Later revisit | Change context to bundles of building materials. | Check whether the captain can use equal groups after a delay. |

All quantities in this example are fictional task data. Their arithmetic is checked; the example is not a new grade-specific placement instrument. Validate activity alignment against existing authoritative curriculum before implementation. The full learning sequence should accommodate the saved starting skill rather than force this script on everyone.

## What to measure and defer

Measure time to the first meaningful attempt, purposeful practice/application time, error→correction→fresh-case performance, hint use, delayed independent performance, and whether the child understands the next action and wants to return. Separate setup/cinematics, instruction, learner work, travel/reward screens, idle time and system waits. Story reading can itself be an ELA task when comprehension is intentionally elicited; ordinary Next clicks should not be counted as academic evidence.

Do not optimize raw question count or speed: a thoughtful explanation may take longer and reveal much more than several guesses. Any proposed time-share target should be a testable design hypothesis, not an efficacy claim. A small formative child playtest can diagnose usability and learning opportunities; it cannot establish market-wide learning outcomes.

Use the existing world as a compact home, relationship context and place where results persist. Exploration earns additional development when it supplies observations, measurements, competing evidence or decisions needed for learning. Decorative walking and additional systems have no priority from this review. Evaluate a shorter or optional introductory path before expanding cinematics; do not cancel previously authorized V05 work or change its runtime here.

Candidate next slices after Ivan's clarification: author the first anchor unit and grade/subject variants (recommended); prototype inquiry bubbles and scene dialogue against that unit; or audit the first 15 minutes of Primer with the measures above. New Rho-invoked work should follow the existing orchestrator-tool/SSE convention. No model change, mastery redesign, new standard codes, deployment or paid assets are proposed by this analysis.
